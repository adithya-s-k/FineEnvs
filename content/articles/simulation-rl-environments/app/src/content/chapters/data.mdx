## Built on real Port of Barcelona data

<HtmlEmbed src="d3-portsim-pipeline.html" frameless />

Each task keeps a real week at one quay and the ships that really called, adds disruptions modelled on real events,
and gets an answer key from CP-SAT. Tasks a simple heuristic nearly solves are dropped: **1,050 train and 50 eval
tasks** remain, and no week appears in both.

**What is measured and what is simulated.** The source records contain the berth plan the port executed,
including ships, dimensions, times and quay sections. They do not contain crane assignments, container manifests
or anchorage waiting times. Workloads, disruptions and physical handling are simulated; the film is a replay of
an agent’s plan, not historical footage of a particular port incident.

### From seed data to tasks

1. **Seed**: the real calls of one quay for one to three weeks, with the berth plan the port actually executed.
2. **Fill the gaps**: crane fleets, wind and traffic rules, and a crane rate of 28 moves per hour turn each call's
   real time alongside into a workload.
3. **Disturb**: inject the disruptions below, scaled by tier (standard, busy, storm, extreme).
4. **Solve**: CP-SAT proves the optimal re-plan, the answer key; the naive and greedy re-plans set the floor.
5. **Filter**: drop tasks a heuristic nearly solves and keep whole weeks out of training for the eval.

| Disruption | What happens | Real-world model | Share of the 1,100 tasks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Late ships | ships arrive hours or days late | Red Sea diversions, [half of ships late](https://www.sea-intelligence.com/press-room/410-global-schedule-reliability-continues-to-plummet-in-august) | 100% |
| Closures | quay sections closed for repairs or dredging | crane-rail repair, fender repair, dredging | 100% |
| Crane outages | cranes out of service for hours or days | breakdowns, maintenance | 89% |
| Priority cargo | some ships count ×3 | transshipment connections | 86% |
| Emergencies | a ship must dock by a deadline | a reefer ship losing power | 76% |
| Extra calls | unscheduled ships need a berth | ad-hoc calls | 63% |
| Gales | no ship moves; ≥300 m stop above 25 kn, all above 30 | the port's [traffic ordinance](https://www.boe.es/diario_boe/txt.php?id=BOE-A-2023-6719) | 62% |
| Bunching | several ships arrive together | delayed services catching up | 46% |
| Diverted traffic | ships moved from the other terminal | terminal outages | 15% |

The film follows one recorded evaluation on quay 24B: **17 ships sharing 9 cranes**. In that task, two cranes are
unavailable during hours 49–83, dredging closes sections 14–22 during hours 104–126, and quay-rail repairs close
sections 9–14 during hours 164–207. These are **simulated operational issues**, defined in the task and enforced by
the checker. The agent must find a schedule that works around them.

The AI badge and animated cursor in the film illustrate the recorded tool calls: draft a plan, call `check_plan`,
edit the rejected assignments, check again, then call `submit_plan`. The evaluated agent sends structured plans
through tools; the cursor makes those changes visible rather than recording a mouse-controlled agent. The full
time–space chart shows every ship, with block width representing time at berth and height representing quay space.

Contains data from the Port de Barcelona open data portal. The extracted data and derived task pack retain the
