## Why it matters

The berth is where a port's capacity is decided: every container on a ship moves only once it is alongside under a
crane, so the berth plan sets how long ships wait, how much fuel they burn at anchor, and whether a whole service
stays on schedule. Idle ships burn money and fuel, and schedules rarely hold, so berth plans keep changing.

Barcelona alone handled [3.9 million TEU from 2,163 container-ship calls](https://www.portdebarcelona.cat/sites/default/files/content/file/2025/02/18/98/portbcntrafic_202412_en.pdf)
in 2024, and in 2025 it [changed its rules](https://www.boe.es/diario_boe/txt.php?id=BOE-A-2025-5157) to give berth
priority to regular services during congestion. Better planning pays: Rotterdam reports its port-call app
[cut waiting by 20%](https://maritime-executive.com/article/rotterdam-introduces-port-call-efficiency-app).
