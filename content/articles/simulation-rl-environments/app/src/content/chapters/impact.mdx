## Why it matters

A ship's containers only move once it is alongside under a crane, so the berth plan decides how long ships wait, how
much fuel they burn at anchor and whether a whole service stays on schedule. Schedules rarely hold, so the plan keeps
changing.

<HtmlEmbed src="d3-portsim-impact.html" frameless />

Barcelona alone handled [3.9 million TEU from 2,163 container-ship calls](https://www.portdebarcelona.cat/sites/default/files/content/file/2025/02/18/98/portbcntrafic_202412_en.pdf)
in 2024, and in 2025 it [changed its rules](https://www.boe.es/diario_boe/txt.php?id=BOE-A-2025-5157) to give berth
priority to regular services during congestion. Rotterdam reports that its port-call app
[cut waiting by 20%](https://maritime-executive.com/article/rotterdam-introduces-port-call-efficiency-app).
