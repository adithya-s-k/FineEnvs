## Conclusions

*Results compiled through 29 September 2026. Article updated 1 October 2026.*

We set out to answer one question: can a small open model be trained inside the harnesses people
actually use, such as Claude Code, Codex and OpenCode, and improve across all of them? It can. The
harnesses run as they ship, with no changes to their code. The capture proxy sits between the
harness and the model and records the exact tokens the model produced. Harbor supplies the tasks and
the sandboxes, and TRL trains on the result.

### What we found

**The harness changes the score before any training.** With the same weights, LFM2.5-2.6B solved
62% of our test tasks under Mini-SWE-Agent and 33% under Claude Code. A score measured in one
harness describes the model in that harness, not the model in general.

**A model gets better where it trains.** This held for both methods we tried. OpenCode-only RL
raised LFM's OpenCode score from 34% to 58%, and OpenCode SFT raised it to 52%. Both did much less in
the other three harnesses. Training across all four spread the gains: multi-harness RL improved
under every harness and ended at 54% overall, against 52% for OpenCode-only RL. That overall gap is
within the noise. The difference is where the gains landed: higher under Claude Code and Codex,
lower in OpenCode.

**The reward decides how long the agent keeps working.** In our earlier Qwen runs, with correctness
as the only reward, nothing told the model to stop, and between 35% and 58% of optimizer steps taught
nothing because every rollout in each group scored the same. A bonus of at most 0.1 for fewer tool calls gave LFM a
signal even when a whole group was correct. The multi-harness LFM model made 31% fewer calls than
the base model on the tasks both solved. There was no LFM run without the bonus, so we can't
separate its effect from the other changes between the Qwen and LFM setups.

**Imitation helped less than RL.** Fine-tuning LFM on a 27B teacher's successful rollouts reached
47.5% with OpenCode data and 43.1% with all four harnesses, both below the two RL runs. Multi-harness
SFT still made 24% fewer tool calls, and fewer in every harness, with nothing in its objective asking
for it. Its overall score, within the noise of the base model, hid gains of 4 to 9 points in three
harnesses and a 17-point drop under Mini-SWE-Agent.

**Exact tokens are only the starting point.** They make the update match what the model generated.
The earlier Qwen runs had exact tokens and still peaked and fell back. One run's answers grew past the
output limit that evaluation allowed, and the other kept calling tools without submitting. The LFM
runs used the same output limit in training and evaluation and a reward that values finishing, and
they did not decline, though they also changed the model and the task pool.

**Steps are a poor unit of comparison.** The two LFM RL runs took the same 1,000 steps, but the
multi-harness run saw 626 distinct tasks against 555 and processed 451M training tokens against
162M. Much of the difference comes from Claude Code, which rewrites its history as it goes, so each
of its rollouts became about eight training rows.

### If you train or ship agent models

- Evaluate in more than one harness, and report each harness as well as the average. An average can
  hide a large drop in one of them.
- Train in the harnesses your users run. Training in one mostly improves that one.
- Give the reward a reason to finish, and use the same output limits in training and evaluation.
- Compare runs by the tasks and tokens they saw, not by optimizer steps.

### What these results can't tell us

Every run used a model of 2 to 2.6 billion parameters and a single seed. The runs in each comparison
differ in more than the one thing we name: token exposure and task coverage differ between harness
mixes, and SFT and RL also differ in objective, data source and compute. The gains over the base
model are large enough to be clear. The 1.9-point overall gap between the two RL runs is not, and
none of the gaps between runs comes from a controlled comparison, so we read them as observations
rather than rankings.

### What comes next

The runs we want next are an LFM run without the efficiency bonus, several seeds per setup, harness
mixes compared at equal token exposure (for SFT, on the 692 tasks the teacher solved in all four
harnesses), SFT followed by RL against RL from the base model, and larger models. Everything here
runs on an open stack: [OpenEnv](https://github.com/huggingface/OpenEnv) for the environments and
the capture proxy, [Harbor](https://harborframework.com/) for tasks and sandboxes,
[TRL](https://github.com/huggingface/trl) for training, and the
[multi-harness SFT data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/FineEnvs/SmolDataEnvs-multiharness-sft). If
you have a harness, a task set or a trainer of your own, you can plug it in and try.
