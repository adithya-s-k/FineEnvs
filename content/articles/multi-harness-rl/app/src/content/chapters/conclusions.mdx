## Conclusions

*Results compiled through 29 September 2026. Article updated 1 October 2026.*

**Multi-harness RL improved both task performance and tool use in our small-model experiments.**
On SmolDataEnvs, LFM2.5-2.6B went from 42.2% to 54.2% pass@1, with gains under all four harnesses:
OpenCode, Claude Code, Codex and Mini-SWE-Agent. Training only in OpenCode also improved the model,
including in other harnesses, but the multi-harness run spread its efficiency gains across all four.
A small open model can learn to work better with several agent interfaces through the same training
pipeline.

The tool-efficiency reward made the way an agent solves a task part of the training objective.
Our earlier Qwen runs rewarded correctness alone, and some learned to make increasingly long
sequences of tool calls. For LFM, we added a small bonus for correct solutions that used fewer
calls. Tool calls fell during training. At the final checkpoint, the multi-harness model used
**31% fewer tool calls on held-out tasks that both it and the base model solved**, with savings
under every harness. The bonus also gave GRPO a learning signal when all scorable rollouts in a
group were correct but took different numbers of calls. Correctness remained essential: a wrong
answer earned no efficiency bonus.

OpenEnv's capture proxy makes this practical without rewriting the harnesses or building a separate
training loop for each one. With a compatible inference backend, it records the exact prompt and
completion tokens, generation log probabilities and loss masks that training needs. Harbor supplies
the tasks and sandboxes, and the same TRL trainer learns from the resulting traces. The harness
keeps its own tools, context management and execution loop. Changing which harness produces the
rollout does not require reimplementing those behaviours inside the trainer.

These are small experiments on one task family, with one seed per setup and unequal data and compute
exposure. They support the gains over the base model, but do not establish a general ranking of
harness mixes. RL produced larger accuracy gains than SFT in these runs; SFT also reduced tool use.
We did not run LFM without the efficiency bonus, so its contribution cannot be separated from the
other changes to the recipe.

The broader opportunity is to train for both successful answers and efficient execution across the
interfaces people actually use. This work provides an open, working path to do that: keep the real
harnesses, capture the evidence needed for learning, and improve the model through its own
interactions with them.
